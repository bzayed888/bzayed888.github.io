Case study · Shining L.I.G.H.T.E Youth Charity

Automating a youth charity's donor, event and contact workflows

My role
Team Lead, CRM Consultant
Team
3 students
Timeline
Apr – Aug 2026
Course
XBIT 4500U Capstone

The client

Shining L.I.G.H.T.E is a youth charity founded in 2023 that supports underprivileged kids through education and empowerment programs. It runs on a small budget with no IT staff.

The challenge

  • Donors who gave through Zeffy got no automatic thank-you or receipt.
  • People who registered for events got no confirmation, so the founder spent time answering "did you get my registration?"
  • Google Form sign-ups sent no confirmation, and kids had no easy way to reach the form.
  • Outlook contacts were saved on one computer only, so they never reached her phone.

How the project changed

Our charter planned evaluation reports on her CRM use. At the first client meeting we learned she already understood her tools; what she needed was hands-on automation, now. We dropped the CRM usage evaluation and put our time into building, testing and documenting the fixes she needed most.

What we delivered

  1. Donor and attendee automation guide. Instant thank-you emails with tax receipts in Zeffy, event e-tickets with a confirmation plus reminders 7 days and 1 day before, and a Mailchimp journey that checks in with donors 6 months after they give.
  2. Google Form and QR code guide. Sign-up forms that email each person a confirmation, plus a QR code kids can scan. We gave two options: a free one in Canva, or a $27.99 lifetime add-on with fewer steps.
  3. Outlook sync guide. Moved contacts from "this computer only" into her Microsoft 365 account so they sync to her iPhone and iPad, with steps for both new and classic Outlook and a troubleshooting section.

Results

  • 5 automations, with no manual follow-up needed for thank-yous, confirmations or reminders.
  • Everything runs on tools the charity already used, at no cost or low cost.
  • Each guide is self-contained, with testing and troubleshooting steps, so no ongoing support is needed.
  • A handover package with recommendations for maintenance and future scaling.

My role as team lead

  • Co-wrote all three guides with my teammates.
  • Split the work so everyone had a clear part and no one was overloaded.
  • Managed the timeline and deliverables, and met the client's needs on schedule.

What I took away

Real clients change plans. Listening at the first meeting, and being willing to drop deliverables that didn't help her, made the project far more useful. Next time I would document scope changes as they happen instead of explaining them at the end.
